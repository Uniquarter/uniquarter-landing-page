What UniQuarter is

Verified students, reviewed listings, confirmed stages

Verified profiles

You verify once, then use that profile for every application.

What this involves
  • Your identity is verified through RealMe or a digital identity provider, and your student status is confirmed against current enrolment each academic year.
  • Sensitive information is released only when you approve it. You can revoke that later, and every release is logged.

Reviewed listings

We review the evidence against published standards before you ever see the listing.

What this involves
  • Evidence of compliance and ownership is uploaded and reviewed before a listing goes live. Nothing publishes on a self-declaration.
  • Photographs are date-stamped, and listings are re-confirmed every year, with reminders for smoke alarm testing in between.

A gated tenancy

Eight stages, each confirmed by both sides before the next one opens.

What this involves
  • Each stage is locked until the one before it is complete, and each is timestamped.
  • Both of you can see where the tenancy stands at any point.

Why we exist

Beds are only half the problem

Students told us they search across websites, agency portals and social media groups, apply over and over, and get turned down with no way of knowing whether it was worth the effort.

Several told us what happened after they finally found somewhere. One lived with a hole in the kitchen floor for three months, through exams, because nobody would deal with it.

Landlords and property managers described the other side of the same process: annual churn, shorter tenancies, screening applicants with no rental history, and doing all of it again every year.

More beds are needed, but the shortage was only half of what we found. The other half is a fragmented process that wastes everyone's time and leaves good landlords indistinguishable from bad ones. We are building a route where every listing's evidence is reviewed before anyone sees it, both parties are verified, and doing the job well earns credit.

Listings are written by the people letting them

The photographs are the ones they chose, and nothing in a listing says how maintenance, bond or the last tenants were handled. Most student renters commit a large share of their income to a property they have seen once, without knowing whether the landlord is any good or the property is what it says it is.

Reputation builds with every tenancy

Every tenancy a student completes adds to their verified history, so the next application doesn't start from zero. A landlord's standing comes from reviews that can only follow a completed tenancy, carried across every property they let. Reviews run both ways on the same terms.

How it works

Eight gated stages, from first viewing to moving in

Stage 1

Property viewing

Book a viewing against times the landlord has published. If you are offshore, you can view virtually.

The gateAttendance is confirmed, by the landlord or, as a fallback, by either party.
Stage 2

Application

Your application is filled in from your verified profile. Apply on your own, or invite people you know and apply for a whole property as one household.

The gateYour details go to that landlord only when you consent, and the release is logged.
Stage 3

Tenant verification

The identity on your verified profile is released to the landlord for this tenancy. You see what is asked for, by whom and for which property before you decide.

The gateA valid consent record exists. Without one, protected information cannot be accessed.
Stage 4

Credit check

A credit check, where wanted, runs under its own separate consent.

The gateA separate consent, or the stage marked not required, for example when the tenancy is guarantor-backed.
Stage 5

Agreement and move-in date

A tenancy agreement template with electronic signature sits inside the process, and the move-in date is agreed at the same point.

The gateBoth parties sign. From here you hold a binding contract, and we will not cancel or expire it.
Stage 6

Condition report

A photo-based record of the property on move-in day, held against the tenancy so both of you have the same evidence at the end.

The gateBoth parties countersign. A report signed by one side is not a record either can rely on.
Stage 7

Bond lodgement

The landlord lodges bond with Tenancy Services under their own credential, and the reference comes back to the tenancy. The deadline is shown with the days remaining.

The gateThe lodgement is confirmed. A self-declared number does not count.
Stage 8

Tenancy start

The process closes and opens a shared record that runs for as long as the tenancy does.

The gateBoth parties confirm the tenancy has begun.

After move-in

We stay with the tenancy once you have the keys

Every issue raised, when it was acted on and when it was resolved stays on the record.

Maintenance log

Raise an issue with photos and a timestamp, and follow it through defined stages to a closure you both confirm.

Reminders and escalation

Reminders repeat to whoever owes the next action, and either of you can escalate to us.

Documents in one place

The agreement, condition report, bond lodgement reference and issue history, with your own copy kept on your side.

Our story

How UniQuarter started

Students should not have to settle for the status quo, and good landlords should not be invisible among the bad ones.

UniQuarter started with Alex Moyse looking at what he could do about the shortage of student accommodation in Christchurch. That took him into the tertiary accommodation sector and to the students living with the problem, who told him about the search, the rejections, and what happened in the properties they eventually got.

It became clear that more accommodation, while necessary, was not the whole answer. A better process could reward the landlords and property managers who do the job well, give students a simplified, verified route into the rental market, and, by making student tenancies less painful to offer, encourage more private owners to put their properties forward.

Alex talked the concept through with his brother Scott early on. Scott recognised it straight away, in part because he had lived it. His own student housing at Swansea University left him with stories that sounded a lot like the ones coming out of Christchurch. This is not a Christchurch problem. Students rent privately in every university town, and they run into the same things everywhere.

Scott came into the company to help build Alex's vision. He has spent years helping businesses develop complex manufacturing processes with off-the-shelf and custom software people can actually use, which is precisely what this needed.

Common questions

Can students use UniQuarter now?

Not yet. A Christchurch pilot is proposed for the 2027 student leasing season.

What does UniQuarter cost a student?

Nothing. Students are never charged.

What does it cost a landlord or property manager?

Nothing to list. We charge a fee only once a tenancy is secured through the platform. Pricing will be published before the pilot opens.

Who can list a property?

Private landlords, property managers and accommodation providers letting under ordinary residential tenancies, once we have reviewed the evidence of compliance and ownership.

Does UniQuarter handle rent or bond money?

No. We never collect or hold money. Rent goes directly between tenant and landlord, and bond is lodged with Tenancy Services.

How is a student verified?

Identity is verified through RealMe for domestic students and a digital identity provider for international students. Student status is confirmed against current enrolment and renewed each academic year.

Does a tertiary provider need to integrate its systems?

No. The platform works without any connection to institutional systems.

Are you competing with property managers or halls?

No. We work alongside private landlords, property managers, on-campus accommodation and purpose-built student housing.
