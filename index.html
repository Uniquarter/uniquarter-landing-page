What UniQuarter is

Trust built into the process, not added afterwards

UniQuarter is a university-integrated trust layer for student housing. We built it around three gates. Each one has to be passed, not simply claimed. Open any of them to see what sits underneath.

Verified profiles

You verify once, then use that profile for every application.

What this involves
  • Identity is verified through RealMe if you are a domestic student, or through a digital identity provider if you are international.
  • Your student status is confirmed separately against your current enrolment, and renewed each academic year.
  • Your verified profile fills in every application, so you never re-enter the same details property by property.
  • Sensitive information is released only when you approve it, and you can revoke that approval later. Every release is logged.
  • If you have never rented before, you are shown honestly as a new renter, with guarantor backing available.

Checked listings

We check the property against published standards before you ever see it.

What this involves
  • Evidence of compliance and of ownership is uploaded and reviewed before a listing goes live. Nothing publishes on a self-declaration.
  • Photographs are date-stamped, so what you see is the property as it is now, not as it was five years ago.
  • Listings are re-confirmed on an annual audit cycle rather than checked once and forgotten.
  • Reminders for smoke alarm testing and compliance reassessment keep the evidence current in between.
  • The same standards apply to everyone listing, whatever their size or their relationship with us.

A gated tenancy

Eight stages, each confirmed by both sides before the next one opens.

What this involves
  • Each stage is locked until the one before it is complete, and each is timestamped.
  • Both of you can see exactly where the tenancy stands at any point. No more wondering whether you have been forgotten.
  • Nothing moves on one party's say-so. Confirmation is required from both sides at every gate.
  • Once you have signed, the tenancy stays in one place for as long as it runs, instead of scattering into email.
  • Reviews open when the tenancy ends, so reputation is earned rather than asserted.

We complement private landlords, property managers, on-campus accommodation and purpose-built student housing rather than competing with them. We serve all students, and we work in housing let under ordinary residential tenancies.

Why we exist

We kept hearing the same thing from both sides

UniQuarter began with a question about the shortage of student accommodation in Christchurch. Looking for an answer put us in front of the people living with it, and what they described was not only a shortage.

Students told us about searching across countless websites, agency portals and social media groups, applying over and over, and being turned down over and over, with no way of knowing whether any of it was worth the effort. Several described what happened after they finally found somewhere. One lived with a hole in the kitchen floor for three months, through exams, because nobody would deal with it.

Landlords and property managers described a different set of problems arising from the same process: annual churn, shorter tenancies, screening applicants who have no rental history behind them, and the cost of doing all of it again every single year.

More beds will help, and more beds are needed. But the shortage was only half of what we found. The other half was a fragmented process that wastes everyone's time and leaves the landlords who do the job well indistinguishable from the ones who do not.

So we set out to build the other half: a route into student housing where the property has been checked before anyone sees it, where both parties are verified, and where doing the job well is something you get credit for.

Two questions, no good way to answer them

What every student is left to work out alone

  • 1 How do I know this is a good landlord? A name on a listing tells you nothing about how maintenance gets handled, how a bond gets settled, or how the last tenants were treated.
  • 2 How do I know the property is what it says it is? The photographs were chosen, and the description was written by the person letting it. Meeting the legal minimum is a floor, not a verdict on whether somewhere is a good place to live.

These are hard questions for anyone, and hardest for someone asking them for the first time. Many student renters are new to renting, some are new to the country, and most are committing a large share of their income to a property they have seen once.

How we hope to serve

Reputation that compounds, on both sides

Trust on UniQuarter is not a badge we hand out. It accumulates, tenancy by tenancy, for students and for landlords alike.

Every tenancy you complete as a student leaves you with a stronger verified history, so your next application does not start from zero. A landlord's standing is built from reviews that can only follow a completed tenancy, visible in full before a student commits, and carried across every property they let.

Reviews run in both directions on identical terms, and neither side can write one without having been through a tenancy with the other. Good landlords get rewarded for being good. Reliable students get recognised for being reliable.

What we are aiming at

For students

A simplified, verified route into the rental market, where the property has been checked and the landlord's track record is visible before you commit to anything.

And the other side of it

For landlords and property managers

Less churn, less administration, applicants who are already verified, and a reputation that works for you across everything you let.

How it works

Eight gated stages, from first viewing to moving in

Select a stage to see what happens and what has to be true before it can close.

Stage 1

Property viewing

You book a viewing against times the landlord has published on a live listing, so the tenancy starts inside the platform rather than in a thread of unanswered messages. If you are offshore, you can view virtually.

The gateAttendance is confirmed before the stage closes, by the landlord as the primary route and by either party as a fallback.
Stage 2

Application

Your application is filled in from your verified profile, so nothing is re-keyed. Apply on your own, or connect by invitation with people you already know and apply for a whole property as one household.

The gateYour personal details and reference details go to that landlord only when you consent at submission, and the release is logged.
Stage 3

Tenant verification

The identity held on your verified profile is released to the landlord for this tenancy. You see what is being asked for, by whom, and for which property, before you decide.

The gateA valid consent record. Protected information cannot be accessed without one. It is not merely hidden from view.
Stage 4

Credit check

Where a credit check is wanted, it runs under its own separate consent rather than being bundled in with anything else. Plenty of student tenancies never need one.

The gateA separate consent, or the stage marked as not required, for example where the tenancy is guarantor-backed.
Stage 5

Agreement and move-in date

A tenancy agreement template with fill-and-sign and electronic signature sits inside the process, and the move-in date is agreed at the same point.

The gateThe agreement is executed by both parties. From here you hold a binding contract, and we will not cancel or expire it.
Stage 6

Condition report

A photo-based record of the property as it stands on the day you move in, held against the tenancy so the same evidence is there for both of you at the end of it.

The gateCountersigned by both parties. A condition report signed by one side is not a record either side can rely on.
Stage 7

Bond lodgement

Bond is lodged with Tenancy Services by the landlord, under their own credential, and the lodgement reference comes back to the tenancy. The lodgement deadline is shown with the days remaining.

The gateA confirmed lodgement, not a self-declared number. Bond goes from the landlord to Tenancy Services and never through us.
Stage 8

Tenancy start

Both of you confirm the tenancy has begun. That closes the process and opens the shared record that runs for as long as the tenancy does.

The gateConfirmation from both sides, as at every other stage.

After the keys

The tenancy does not fall off the end of the process

Most platforms stop at the introduction. We hold the tenancy in one place for as long as it runs, so what was raised, when it was acted on and when it was resolved is on the record rather than in somebody's recollection.

Maintenance log

Raise an issue with photos and a timestamp, then watch it move through defined stages to a closure you both confirm.

Reminders and escalation

Reminders repeat to whoever owes the next action, and either of you can escalate to us when something is not being dealt with.

Documents in one place

The agreement, the condition report, the bond lodgement reference and the issue history, all together, with your own copy kept on your side.

Two-way reviews

Opened when the tenancy ends, so the next student sees what the last one experienced and every good landlord builds a track record.

Our story

It started with a shortage, and turned into something bigger

Students should not have to settle for the status quo, and good landlords should not be invisible among the bad ones.

UniQuarter started with Alex Moyse looking at what he could do about the shortage of student accommodation in Christchurch. That work took him into the tertiary accommodation sector directly, and through it to the students living with the problem. They talked about the search, the endless applications, the rejections, and what happened in the properties they eventually managed to get.

It became clear that building more accommodation, while necessary, was not going to be the whole answer. Sitting alongside the shortage was a process problem. Students were searching across countless mediums and applying endlessly through multiple agency portals, often just to be rejected. Landlords and property managers were carrying annual churn, shorter tenancies, and the cost of screening applicants with nothing behind them.

The idea that came out of it was that a better process could do something more buildings on their own cannot. It could reward the landlords and property managers who do the job well. It could give students a simplified, verified route into the rental market. And by making student tenancies less painful to offer, it could encourage more private owners to put their properties forward in the first place.

Alex talked the concept through with his brother Scott early on. Scott recognised it straight away, in part because he had lived it. His own student housing while he was at Swansea University had left him with a set of stories that sounded a lot like the ones coming out of Christchurch, which made something clear fairly quickly: this is not a Christchurch problem. Students rent privately in every university town there is, and they run into the same things everywhere. What was being designed here would be worth having well beyond this city.

Scott came into the company to build it with him. He has spent years turning complicated physical processes into software people can actually use, which is precisely what this needed.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can students use UniQuarter now?

Not yet. We are still building. A Christchurch pilot is proposed for the 2027 student leasing season, and registering interest is the way to hear when it opens.

What does UniQuarter cost a student?

Nothing. Students are never charged to use UniQuarter.

What does it cost a landlord or property manager?

Nothing to list. We charge a fee only once a tenancy is secured through the platform, so there is no cost in putting a property in front of students. Pricing will be published before the pilot opens.

Who can list a property?

Private landlords, property managers and accommodation providers letting under ordinary residential tenancies, once we have reviewed the evidence of compliance and of ownership. Nothing goes live on a self-declaration.

Does UniQuarter handle rent or bond money?

No. We never collect or hold money. Rent goes directly between tenant and landlord, and bond is lodged with Tenancy Services by the landlord.

How is a student verified?

Identity is verified through RealMe for domestic students and through a digital identity provider for international students. Student status is confirmed separately against current enrolment and renewed each academic year.

Does a tertiary provider need to integrate its systems?

No. The platform works without any connection to institutional systems and without any transfer of student personal information from the institution.

Are you competing with property managers or halls?

No. We complement private landlords, property managers, on-campus accommodation and purpose-built student housing. We are adding a verified, checked layer to the private rental market, not replacing what already works.
